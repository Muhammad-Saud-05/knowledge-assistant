## Question sets: what was frozen and when

- **v1** is the original 40 questions (q01 to q40). It was frozen before any baseline was run, and the v1
  baseline was run and read on it.
- **v2** is v1 plus 15 questions (q41 to q55). **The 15 additions were written after the v1 baseline had
  been seen.** They are therefore not blind: the author knew which kinds of questions each mode had
  struggled with. They are marked `added_in: "v2"` in `eval/questions.json`, and the first 40 entries are
  unchanged from v1.
- Re-running the v1 questions inside the v2 run gave identical rankings for all 93 (question, mode)
  pairs, so the v1 numbers below are the same numbers the v1 baseline produced.

## Setup

- Database: 30 documents, 502 chunks (4 original notes in `sample_corpus/`, 26 third-party documents
  whose sources and licences are in `docs/corpus_manifest.csv`).
- Embeddings: `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, 384 dimensions, cosine distance.
- Modes: **vector** (nearest chunks by meaning), **keyword** (PostgreSQL full-text search, OR semantics,
  `ts_rank_cd`), **hybrid** (30 candidates from each method fused with reciprocal rank fusion, k = 60).
  No reranker in the baseline (Phase 2 adds one; see below).
- Each question is searched in each mode with its filters, and the top 10 chunks are judged.
- Question file `eval/questions.json`, sha256 `d1cf32202152...`. The run was made from commit `37d1784`
  with the v2 changes not yet committed.

Four single-document questions list an `also_acceptable` passage found by text search (q07, q12, q20,

low-overlap and 20 high-overlap.

The four filtered questions are **not** in the main results; they are a check that filters work and have
their own table. The unanswerable questions have no retrieval metric; only their top-1 scores are
recorded.

The per-document limit was raised from 3 to 4 for v2, because q54 uses `fastapi-middleware.md` a fourth
time (with q01, q25, q26). The v1 questions were not touched.

## Metric definitions

- A chunk is **relevant** if it comes from an expected document (or, for a single-document question,
  an `also_acceptable` one) and contains at least one of that document's evidence phrases, after
  normalisation (lowercase, whitespace collapsed, markdown emphasis characters and underscores removed,
  curly quotes made plain). This is strict and mechanical: another passage that also answers the
  question counts as a miss unless it was listed.
- **hit@k**: a relevant chunk in the top k. For a multi-document question this needs a relevant chunk
  from *every* expected document within the top k.
- **MRR@10**: reciprocal rank of the first relevant chunk in the top 10 (0 if none).
- **doc_hit@5**: any top-5 chunk from an expected document (the lenient measure: right document, not
  necessarily the right passage).
- **multi_doc coverage@5**: the share of a multi-document question's documents with a relevant chunk in
  the top 5, averaged over those questions.
- Counts are always shown next to percentages (for example 15/23). Intervals are percentile bootstrap
  95% intervals (1000 resamples, seed 7). The **paired** intervals resample the same questions for both
  modes and bootstrap the per-question difference, so they show whether a difference is real; two
  separate intervals that overlap cannot.
- Retrieval time is the time for one search call, including embedding the question, on this machine.

## What the data shows

Only what the numbers support, on 30 development questions in the main table (8 of them multi-document).

- **Hybrid beats keyword on this question set.** The paired difference is clear on both measures: hit@5
  +0.300 (95% interval 0.10 to 0.50) and MRR@10 +0.271 (0.15 to 0.40). Keyword missed 21 of 30 at k=5.
- **Hybrid against vector is not settled.** Hybrid has the higher hit@5 (18/30 against 17/30; paired
  difference +0.033, interval -0.13 to 0.20) and vector has the higher hit@1 (10/30 against 6/30) and
  MRR@10 (0.586 against 0.483; paired difference -0.103, interval -0.23 to 0.03). Neither interval
  excludes zero. The modes differ on only 7 questions at hit@5 (4 for hybrid, 3 for vector). Hybrid
  finds a relevant chunk in the top 5 about as often, but places it first less often; on MRR it looks
  worse than a single mode, though the evidence is too thin to call that real. The sign of the MRR
  difference is negative in every category (exact_term -0.120, paraphrase -0.106, multi_doc -0.081),
  and every one of those intervals includes zero.
- **The additions are harder than v1 for keyword and hybrid, not for vector.** On the 7 added main-table
  questions, hit@5 is 5/7 for vector, 0/7 for keyword and 3/7 for hybrid. Adding them moved hybrid from
  15/23 to 18/30 (65.2% to 60.0%) and MRR@10 from 0.517 to 0.483, and keyword from 39.1% to 30.0%;
  vector barely moved (52.2% to 56.7%, MRR 0.584 to 0.586). Seven questions is a small group, and they
  were written after the v1 baseline had been seen, so this says little about how the modes compare in
  general.
- **Retrieval usually finds the right document but not the right passage.** doc_hit@5 is 86.7% for
  vector, 76.7% for keyword and 93.3% for hybrid, while hit@5 is 56.7%, 30.0% and 60.0%.
- **Multi-document questions are hard for every mode.** hit@5 is 1/8 for vector, 0/8 for keyword and 2/8
  for hybrid; mean coverage@5 is 0.38, 0.19 and 0.50. Six of the eight (q23, q27, q28, q29, q53, q54)
  were missed by all three modes.
- **Low-overlap questions.** There are 10 in the main table. Vector reaches hit@5 on 6/10, hybrid on
  5/10 and keyword on 3/10. Hybrid minus vector on MRR@10 is -0.164 for this bucket, with an interval
  (-0.44 to 0.14) that includes zero.
- **Filters work.** The three filtered development questions returned only documents satisfying their
  filters (0 violations) and all three were hit at k=5 in every mode.
- **Top-1 scores do not separate unanswerable from answerable questions in this sample.** Vector cosine
  for the nine unanswerable development questions ranged from 0.700 to 0.792, inside the answerable range
  (0.637 to 0.894). Keyword and hybrid scores overlap in the same way. No threshold has been chosen.
- **Speed:** median search time per question was 56 ms for vector, 13 ms for keyword and 71 ms for
  hybrid (95th percentile 70, 38 and 108 ms).

## Limitations

- **The additions were written after the v1 baseline had been read.** Treat the v2 numbers as less
  independent than v1. Differences between v1 and v2 results can reflect how the new questions were
  chosen, not a change in the system.
- **Small sample.** 30 questions in the main table and 10 or fewer in many rows. The confidence
  intervals are wide, most differences between vector and hybrid are not distinguishable from zero,
  and changing a single question's label can move a percentage by several points.
- **Questions are not independent.** q15 and q23 (and q28) share passages; q23 and q27 both use the
  Docker PDF's deployment advice; q28 is built from passages also covered, more generally, by the
  system-design primer. Among the additions, q41 and q53 rest on the same passage of the async page,
  q09 and q54 on the same 429 passage, and q54 shares `fastapi-middleware.md` with q01, q25 and q26. A
  single retrieval miss can therefore cost several questions at once, and the bootstrap, which resamples
  questions as if independent, understates the uncertainty.
- **Some labels are judgement calls.** q48 (Ollama on a Raspberry Pi) is unanswerable, but the FastAPI
  Docker PDF mentions Raspberry Pi in another context. q52 (Python version to build Ollama) is treated
  as unanswerable because the build prerequisites list Go, CMake and a C/C++ compiler and no Python,
  but a reader could argue the answer is "none". For q55, the testing page supplies the installation
  and `TestClient` part and the error-handling page supplies the 404 and the message; neither page alone
  shows a test asserting a 404.
- **Written for this corpus.** The questions were written against this specific, documentation-heavy
  corpus by the person who built the system, after reading the passages. Results may not carry over to
  other kinds of documents or to differently phrased questions.
- **Relevance is judged by evidence phrases.** It is mechanical and strict. Passages that answer a
  question but were not labelled (outside the four `also_acceptable` additions) count as misses, so
  hit rates are probably lower bounds. Word-level overlap is also only a rough proxy for how hard a
  question is.
- **Dates.** `doc_date` for the third-party documents is the file's modified date (2026-11-14), not a
  publication date. Only the author's own notes carry real front-matter dates, so the date-filter
  questions (q39 and q40) work only on those.
- **The holdout has now been run once** (see "Holdout (run once)"), for A and C only, and is spent: it
  must not be used again to choose between configurations.
- **Timing** comes from a single run on one machine and varies from run to run.
- **The Phase 2b decision rule had no latency limit and no bound on ranking quality.** It selected a configuration
  (L) that was about 10 s slower per question and lower on MRR@10 for low-overlap questions; see the addendum of

## Phase 2 pre-registration

Written and committed **before any Phase 2 configuration was run**. Nothing below may be changed after
results are seen; anything learned later goes in a separate, labelled section.

**Data.** Question set v2, development split only (the 13 holdout questions stay locked; `--confirm-holdout`
is not used). No question, label, split or the question file is changed in Phase 2. Suspected label
problems are listed under "Suspected label issues" and not fixed.

**Reranker (fixed, not tuned).** `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` through sentence-transformers'
CrossEncoder on CPU, `max_length` 512, input style `plain` (chunk text only), raw model output kept as
the score (no sigmoid applied by the code). The Phase 2 plan also lists `BAAI/bge-reranker-base` and a
`with_context` input style; they are **not run** here because this phase is limited to five
configurations. The 30 best fused candidates per query go into the reranker. The runner keeps the
reranked top 10 so that MRR@10 can be computed; hit@k for k up to 5 is read from the top of that list,
which equals the reranked top 5.

**Configurations (five, no more).**

| id | configuration | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| A | hybrid | the current baseline: 30 vector + 30 Postgres full-text candidates, reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60) |
| B | hybrid + reranker | A's top 30 fused candidates, reranked |
| C | vector + reranker | the top 30 vector candidates, reranked |
| D | hybrid, BM25 leg | as A, but the Postgres full-text leg is replaced by BM25 (`rank_bm25`, BM25Okapi, default k1 = 1.5, b = 0.75) over chunk text loaded from the database; the same filters apply; the Postgres full-text code stays in place |
| E | D + reranker | D's top 30 fused candidates, reranked |

**Decision rule.** Among A to E, take the best dev **MRR@10** in the main table (exact_term, paraphrase,
multi_doc; n = 30). Every configuration whose MRR@10 is within 0.02 of the best is a tie, and among tied
configurations the one with the lowest **median** retrieval time per question is chosen. If A is chosen,
nothing changes.

**What is reported.** For every configuration: the main table, by category, by difficulty bucket (with n),
the missed-at-k list, and median and p95 time per question including the reranker (plus the reranker's
size and load time on this CPU). Paired bootstrap differences against A for every configuration (hit@5,
MRR@10, hit@1, doc_hit@5; 1000 resamples, seed 7).

**Calibration (analysis only; no threshold goes into the app).** Top-1 score of each dev question for the
selected configuration and for A, answerable versus unanswerable (9 unanswerable dev questions, so very
thin), with a table of thresholds showing the share of unanswerable questions refused against the share
of answerable questions wrongly refused. If the groups cannot be separated, that is stated.

**Not done in this step.** Holdout run (only on request, once, for A and the selected configuration), the
API/UI defaults, and Phase 3.

### What the reranker changed

- **Ranking quality improved clearly; hit@5 improved modestly.** Against A, MRR@10 rose by +0.189 (B),
  +0.209 (C) and +0.224 (E), and each paired interval excludes zero (lowest bound +0.06). hit@5 rose by
  +0.133, +0.100 and +0.133 with intervals that include or touch zero (not clear), and hit@1 by +0.167,
  +0.200 and +0.200 (not clear). The reranker mostly moved a relevant chunk that was already in the
  top 10 up to the top, rather than finding passages the first stage had missed (doc_hit@5 rose by only
  one or two questions).
- **By category.** The MRR@10 gain is largest for multi_doc (A 0.565 to 0.854 for B, C and E; the paired
  interval excludes zero) and exact_term (0.483 to 0.792; interval includes zero with 8 questions), and
  smallest for paraphrase (0.437 to 0.501 for B, 0.544 for C, 0.574 for E; includes zero).
- **It also lost one.** q03 is a hit at 5 under A but not under B, C or E: the passage that mentions
  `.add_task()` (chunk 85) fell out of the top 10, and the reranker preferred other chunks of the right
  document. Four questions were missed by every reranked configuration and by A: q27, q28, q29, q54.
- **The BM25 leg alone (D)** did not change hit@5 (18/30 for A and D, 4 better and 4 worse), raised hit@1
  by +0.200 (interval 0.03 to 0.37) and MRR@10 by +0.128 (interval -0.01 to 0.26, not clear), and
  gave doc_hit@5 30/30. Replacing the leg and adding the reranker (E) was not clearly better than
  the reranker alone (B or C).
- **The cost is time.** Median search time per question went from 92 ms (A) to 2.9 to 3.2 s with the
  reranker (95th percentile 4.4 to 5.1 s, above the 3 s level the plan treats as a reason to prefer a
  faster setup), almost all of it spent scoring 30 passages on the CPU. The three reranked
  configurations differ by a few hundred milliseconds, within run-to-run variation. This is added in
  front of the language model's own response time.
- **Stability across question sets.** On the 23 v1 questions the reranked configurations score 69.6% to
  78.3% at hit@5 and on the 7 added ones 4/7 to 5/7, against 3/7 for A, so the improvement was not
  confined to the questions that were written after the first baseline.
- **Risk of overfitting.** Everything above comes from 30 main-table questions, several of which share
  passages (see Limitations), and the choice among five configurations was made on those same
  questions. The holdout has not been run; with 13 questions it could only confirm a direction.

## Application latency with the reranker wired in

Measured through the running API (`POST /api/ask`, `uvicorn` on port 8011, one question at a time, the
local `llama3.2:3b` model). **How:** a script sent each question and timed, on the client, the interval from
sending the request to the arrival of the first `token` event. The server was started twice, once with
`RERANKER_ENABLED=false` (plain hybrid, configuration A) and once with the reranker (configuration C). In
each run one warm-up question (not counted) loaded the language model, then the same five new questions
(none is an evaluation question) were asked once each, in the same order, all of one configuration before
the other so that no question followed an identical prompt. The server's own retrieval time comes from the
`done` event.

**Reading it.**

- The reranker adds about 2.4 s of retrieval time (median 0.14 s to 2.55 s), consistent with the 2.9 s
  median in the dev evaluation.
- The median time to first token was nonetheless 2.6 s *lower* with the reranker (31.3 s against 33.9 s).
  That is not a speed-up from reranking. The first token waits mostly for the language model to read the
  prompt, which grows with the length of the sources: the slowest runs are the ones with the longest sources
  (10,050 characters took 72 s; 2,612 took 21.5 s). On these five questions the reranked top five happened
  to be shorter than hybrid's (median 4165 against 5910 characters), which more than offset the 2.4 s.
- Five questions on one machine, one run each, cannot separate a 2.4 s cost from this variation (the range
  was 28 to 72 s without the reranker and 22 to 43 s with it). The honest statement is: reranking costs about
  2.4 s of retrieval time, and its effect on the time to the first token was not distinguishable from the
  variation caused by prompt length.
- The sources appear in the `sources` event after about 0.8 s without the reranker and about 3.4 s with it.

## Phase 2b pre-registration

Written and committed **before any Phase 2b code was written or run**. Every decision in this phase is
made on the **development split only**; the holdout has been run once (Phase 2) and is spent, so it is
not run again. No question, label, split or the question file is changed. Retrieval-only evaluation on the
dev main table (exact_term, paraphrase, multi_doc; n = 30, of which 8 multi_doc).

### Part 1: question decomposition (retrieval only)

**Configurations.**

| id | configuration |
|---|---|
| C | the current default: vector top 30, rerank, top 5 (top 10 kept for MRR@10) |
| R | rule-based split (below); a question that is not split is handled exactly as in C |
| L | LLM split with `llama3.2:3b` (below); a question that is not split is handled exactly as in C |

**R, the rule (fixed here, not tuned afterwards).** The question text is cut into parts at (a) a question
mark that is followed by more text, (b) a semicolon, and (c) the word "and" when it is immediately followed
by one of: how, what, which, why, when, where, who, can, could, should, do, does, is, are, will, would
(case-insensitive). Each part is trimmed of spaces, commas, semicolons and a leading "and"; a part that
lacks a final question mark gets one if the original question ended with one. Parts of fewer than 3 words
are discarded. If fewer than 2 parts remain, the question is **not split**; if more than 3 remain, only the
first 3 are used. No other rewriting is done, so a sub-question may lack context that was in the original.

**L, the LLM split.** `llama3.2:3b` through Ollama, temperature 0, JSON output mode, the same 4096-token
context window as the answer model. System prompt (exact text):

> You split a user's question into standalone sub-questions. If the question asks for one thing, return it
> unchanged as the only sub-question. If it asks for two or more different things, return at most 3
> sub-questions, each of which can be understood on its own (repeat any names it needs). Reply with JSON
> only, in the form {"sub_questions": ["...", "..."]}.

The user message is the question. The reply is valid only if it is a JSON object whose `sub_questions` is a
list of 1 to 3 non-empty strings of at most 300 characters each. **Any other reply, any parse failure and any
error from the model falls back to the original question.** If exactly one sub-question is returned, the
original question is used unchanged. The prompt is not changed after the first run.

**Retrieval for R and L.** For each sub-question: vector top 30, reranked against that sub-question with the
same reranker as C. The lists are merged **round-robin**: take the best chunk of the first list, then of the
second, then of the third, and repeat, skipping a chunk already taken (de-duplicated by chunk id), until 10
chunks are collected or the lists run out. Reranker scores are never compared across sub-questions. The
first 5 of the merged list are the top 5. One run on the dev split for each of C, R and L.

**Decision rule.** Adopt R or L only if **both**: (1) dev multi_doc coverage@5 is at least **0.15 higher**
than C's, and (2) **no category's dev hit@5 falls by more than one question** below C's (categories:
exact_term, paraphrase, multi_doc). If both R and L qualify, choose the one with the lower median time per
question (the time includes the decomposition). If neither qualifies, C stays the default, nothing in the
defaults changes, and the negative result is reported.

**Reported.** The same tables as before (main table, by category, missed-at-k, paired differences against C
for hit@5, MRR@10 and multi_doc coverage@5), the median decomposition time for L, and for each dev
multi_doc question the sub-questions R and L produced.

### Part 2: time to first token against context size

**Settings** (all on configuration C's ranking, whatever Part 1 decides, so that only the amount of source
text differs):

| id | the sources put into the prompt |
|---|---|
| k3 | the top 3 chunks |
| k4 | the top 4 chunks |
| k5 | the top 5 chunks (the current default) |
| B4500 | up to 5 chunks within a total of 4,500 characters of chunk text: starting from the top 5, drop the lowest-ranked chunk while the total exceeds 4,500 characters (at least one chunk is always kept) |

**Retrieval quality of each setting** (dev main table, from the saved Phase 2 rankings of

**Time to first token.** The same 5 non-evaluation questions as in the earlier latency measurement, each

### What the result does and does not show

- **Coverage and multi-doc hit@5 improved for L.** multi_doc coverage@5 rose from 0.750 to 0.938 (paired
  +0.188, interval 0.06 to 0.38, 3 questions better and none worse of 8) and multi_doc hit@5 from 4/8 to 7/8
  (paired +0.375, interval 0.12 to 0.75). Overall hit@5 rose from 21/30 to 23/30 (+0.067, interval -0.10 to
  0.23, not clear). q27, q28 and q29 went from misses to hits.
- **Ranking quality got worse, not clearly.** MRR@10 fell from 0.693 to 0.632 (paired -0.061, interval
  -0.15 to 0.03) and hit@1 from 12/30 to 10/30 (paired -0.067, interval -0.17 to 0.00). On the
  low-overlap questions the MRR@10 drop is -0.173 with an interval that excludes zero (-0.38 to 0.00, 10
  questions), the one clearly negative result in the table.
- **Two questions that C answered were lost:** q45 and q46 (paraphrase, both added in v2) are misses under L.
  Paraphrase hit@5 fell from 10/14 to 9/14, and q38, a filtered question, went from rank 1 to rank 4.
- **L split almost everything.** 28 of the 30 main-table questions were split, including questions that ask
  one thing. The prompt says to return a single-ask question unchanged, but `llama3.2:3b` often returned
  several sub-questions anyway. q25 became three sub-questions, one of which ("How do I serve a frontend
  from http://localhost:8080?") is not what was asked. For q53 and q54 the sub-questions are paraphrases
  that lose context ("What is Ollama's chat REST endpoint URL?"). q54 is still missed.
- **R split 7 of 30** (the clearly two-part questions) and changed little: coverage +0.062 (not clear),
  hit@5 unchanged (21/30), MRR@10 -0.011. Two questions gained (q27, q29) and two were lost (q41, q53).
  Sub-questions after the first part lose the context of the first (for example "how should I declare my
  endpoint function?").
- **Cost.** L's median decomposition time was 4.4 s (95th percentile 8.0 s) and the median total time per
  question rose from 2.7 s (C) to 12.5 s (95th percentile 18.7 s): the model call plus one rerank of 30
  passages per sub-question. R costs nothing measurable at the median (2.7 s) but its 95th percentile
  was 6.5 s, since split questions rerank twice.
- **Small numbers.** The coverage result rests on 8 multi_doc questions; the questions q23, q24, q27 and
  q29 have two natural halves that the model split cleanly, and several share passages (see Limitations).
  The sub-question lists above can be read to judge whether each split is sensible.

## Adopting L in the application

*Superseded on 2026-11-14: L was made the default for a few hours and then reverted; see the addendum below. The description that follows is what was done at the time.*

Because the rule adopted L, it is the default for `ask`, `/api/ask` (mode `default`) and the CLI, with
`DECOMPOSITION_ENABLED=false` returning to C, and `RERANKER_ENABLED=false` returning to plain hybrid. The
merged list is what the `sources` event carries and what citations are validated against; the model still
answers the original question. A failure to split (Ollama unreachable, a malformed reply) falls back to
searching with the whole question.

## Addendum, 2026-11-14: why L is not the default

The pre-registered rule selected L, and that verdict stands as written above: L met both conditions. After
seeing the full result, the default was nevertheless kept at C (vector top 30, rerank, top 5 into the prompt;
`DECOMPOSITION_ENABLED` defaults to `false`). L stays available as an opt-in: the API mode `rerank-split-llm`
works whatever the setting is, the web app has an unchecked-by-default option "Compare across documents
(slower)" that sends it, and `DECOMPOSITION_ENABLED=true` makes it the default. The reasons:

- The rule had **no latency limit**, and L added about **10 s per question** (median search time 12.5 s
  against 2.7 s for C), on top of a first word that already takes tens of seconds on this CPU.
- L **lowered MRR@10 on the low-overlap questions** by -0.173, a paired interval that excludes zero
  (-0.38 to 0.00, 10 questions).
- The model **split 28 of 30 questions**, including questions that ask a single thing, so most of its cost
  is paid by questions that gain nothing.

**This is a limitation of the decision rule.** The rule asked only for a coverage gain and for no category to
lose more than one question at hit@5. It set no bound on time per question and none on MRR@10 or hit@1, so
a configuration could pass it while being much slower and worse at ranking. The default was therefore chosen
by judgement after the result was seen, not by the rule, and the rule's verdict has not been edited. A better
rule would have included a latency limit and a no-worse condition on MRR@10. The holdout was not used to
test L (it had already been run for A and C, and L was selected on development data).
